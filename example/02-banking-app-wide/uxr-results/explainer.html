What we measured

We tested two designs of the mobile banking app: Current and Redesign. Ten people tried each design, and each person saw only one of them. Everyone did the same four tasks: send money to a friend, freeze a card, find last month's statement, and set up a savings goal.

For each task we recorded whether the person finished, how long it took, and how easy they found it. After each task, people rated how easy it was from 1 to 7. A task counted as finished only when the person completed all of it on their own. Tasks left half done, or finished with help, counted as not finished.

Your result

Each design gets one usability score from 0 to 100%. A score of 50% means exactly on target. Above 50% is better than the target, and below 50% is worse.

The Redesign scored 66%, and its range runs from 54% to 79%. Even the low end of that range is above the target, so the Redesign is clearly above target.

The Current design scored 53%, with a range from 27% to 74%. That range includes the 50% target, so we cannot yet tell whether the Current design is above or below it.

The Redesign scored 13 points higher than the Current design. The true difference most likely lies between 10 points lower and 43 points higher. That range includes no difference at all, so we cannot yet tell which design is better.

What the score is made of

Each task score has three equal parts: how many people finished, how fast they were, and how easy they found it. Each task has its own time target. The ease target was an average rating of 5.5 out of 7. Finishing had no required rate, so it has no target.

Freezing a card scored 77% in both designs, clearly above target in each. The other three tasks each scored higher in the Redesign. For those three, we cannot yet tell whether either design is above or below target.

  • Send money to a friend (Current 53%, Redesign 63%): In the Current design, 6 of 10 people finished, in a typical 66 seconds against a target of 90 seconds. The average ease rating was 4.56, below the 5.5 target. In the Redesign, 8 of 10 finished, in a typical 79 seconds, still within target. Ease was 5.29, still below target.
  • Freeze your card (Current 77%, Redesign 77%): In the Current design, 9 of 10 people finished, in a typical 23 seconds against a target of 30 seconds. Ease was 5.88, which meets the target. In the Redesign, all 10 of 10 finished, in a typical 25 seconds. Ease was 5.6, which also meets the target.
  • Find last month's statement (Current 37%, Redesign 61%): In the Current design, only 4 of 10 people finished, in a typical 58 seconds against a target of 60 seconds. Ease was 4.2, below target. In the Redesign, 9 of 10 finished. But a typical attempt took 64 seconds, just over the target, and ease was 5.3, below target.
  • Set up a savings goal (Current 43%, Redesign 60%): In the Current design, 4 of 10 people finished, in a typical 143 seconds against a target of 150 seconds. Ease was 4.67, below target. In the Redesign, 7 of 10 finished, in a typical 138 seconds. Ease was 5.56, which meets the target.

How sure we are

With ten people per design, every score comes with a wide range. The true score for the Current design most likely lies between 27% and 74%. For the Redesign, it most likely lies between 54% and 79%.

The two ranges overlap a lot. That is why a 13-point lead is not yet enough to call the Redesign better. Testing more people per design would narrow the ranges and show whether the lead is real.

What to fix first

In both designs, the weakest part of every task was how easy people found it. In the Current design, ease was below target for every task except freezing a card. The lowest rating was 4.2, for finding last month's statement.

The Current design also lost many people before the end. Only 4 of 10 found last month's statement, and only 4 of 10 set up a savings goal. Start with the recordings of these two tasks.

In the Redesign, ease was still below target for sending money (5.29) and for finding the statement (5.3). Finding the statement also took a little longer than its target. Look into these two tasks next in the Redesign.

What this number cannot tell you

The scores show where people struggled, not why. To find the reasons, watch the session recordings or talk to participants. The results also cover only these four tasks and the people who took part.

Time counts only the people who finished a task. People who gave up are left out, so the times can look better than the experience really was.

Ease ratings come only from the people who were asked the ease question. Where we did not ask it, we left the rating blank and did not count it.

Every Redesign participant froze their card. A perfect result like that cannot be placed on the scoring scale as it is. So we applied a small standard correction to every finish rate in the study. It counts each result as if half a success and half a failure were added.

This nudges every finish rate slightly toward the middle: 10 of 10 counts as 95%, and 6 of 10 as 59%. We chose this before anyone saw the scores. Because of it, the scores differ slightly from what the original method would give.

We left out one pilot session, because it was a trial run and not part of the study. Another common way of scoring gives a similar picture: 51% for the Current design and 61% for the Redesign.

Glossary

Usability score
One number from 0 to 100% that sums up how many people finished, how fast, and how easy they found it. 50% means exactly on target.
Target
The level agreed before the study: a time for each task and an average ease rating of 5.5 out of 7.
Range
The span in which the true value most likely lies; testing fewer people gives a wider range.
Ease rating
The answer to a question asked after each task about how easy it was, from 1 (very hard) to 7 (very easy).
Typical time
A middle value for how long people who finished took, so one unusually fast or slow person does not distort it.
Finish rate
The share of people who fully finished a task on their own, for example 6 of 10.
Small correction (Laplace smoothing)
A standard adjustment that counts every result as if half a success and half a failure were added. It lets a perfect 10 of 10 be scored and moves every finish rate slightly toward the middle.
