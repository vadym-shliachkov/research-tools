What we measured

We asked 15 people to do four tasks on the hotel booking site. They searched for a hotel, chose a room, paid, and cancelled a booking.

For each task we noted three things. Did the person finish? How long did it take? And how easy did it feel, on a scale from 1 to 7?

We also noted whether each person used a computer or a phone. We did not split the results by device.

This is the first round of a study we will repeat. The next round will use the same method and targets, so we can compare the two.

Your result

The overall usability score is 46%. On this scale, 50% means exactly on target. Higher is better, and lower is worse.

The score looks a little below the 50% target, but we cannot tell yet. The likely range runs from 33% to 64%, and that includes the target.

The same is true for each of the four tasks. Every task score is close to 50%, and every task's likely range includes the target.

What the score is made of

Each task score combines three parts: finishing the task, the time it took, and the ease rating.

For finishing, the team agreed in advance that 78% of people should finish. A task where exactly 78% finish counts as on target. So the score rewards meeting that bar, not beating an average.

No task reached the 78% bar. Every task was quicker than its time target. On every task, the average ease rating was below the target of 5.5.

  • Search for a hotel (42%): 7 of 15 people finished. The typical time was 64 seconds, against a target of 90 seconds. The average ease rating was 4.33 out of 7.
  • Choose a room (47%): 9 of 15 people finished. The typical time was 52 seconds, against a target of 60 seconds. The average ease rating was 4.87 out of 7.
  • Pay (51%): 6 of 15 people finished. The typical time was 78 seconds, against a target of 120 seconds. The average ease rating was 4.6 out of 7.
  • Cancel a booking (44%): 8 of 15 people finished. The typical time was 73 seconds, against a target of 90 seconds. The average ease rating was 4.8 out of 7.

How sure we are

With only 15 people, the true overall score is most likely between 33% and 64%. That range is wide enough to include the target.

Each task on its own is even less certain. The payment task, for example, could score anywhere from 36% to 84%.

Testing with more people in the next round would narrow these ranges. Then we could say whether the site meets its target.

What to fix first

For searching, choosing a room and cancelling, the weakest part is ease. People who finished were quick, but they did not find these tasks easy.

Searching for a hotel had the lowest ease rating, 4.33 out of 7. Start there to find out what made people work hard.

For payment, the weakest part is finishing: only 6 of 15 people completed it. Before changing anything, review where people stopped during payment.

What this number cannot tell you

The score shows how well people managed the tasks, not why they struggled. To learn why, look at the session notes or recordings.

Times count only the people who finished. People who gave up are not in the times: 8 for searching, 6 for choosing a room, 9 for payment and 7 for cancelling. So the good times describe only the people who got through.

We did not compare computer and phone users, so the score may hide a difference between them. The results also cover only these four tasks and these 15 people.

As a first round, this score is a starting point. It becomes more useful once the next round, measured the same way, can sit beside it.

Glossary

Usability score
One number from 0 to 100% that sums up finishing, time and ease. 50% means exactly on target.
Task score
The same kind of score for a single task, built from that task's finishing, time and ease.
Target
The level agreed before the study. For finishing, it is 78% of people; for time and ease, each task has its own target.
Likely range
The span where the true score most likely lies; a small study gives a wide range.
Typical time
One time that sums up how long the task usually took the people who finished it.
Ease rating
One question after each task: how easy was it, from 1 (very hard) to 7 (very easy).
Round
One run of a study that is repeated over time with the same method, so results can be compared.
