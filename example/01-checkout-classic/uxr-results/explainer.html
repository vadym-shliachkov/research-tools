What we measured

12 people tried three tasks in the online shop. They checked out an item, found the nearest store and started a return.

For each task, we noted whether the person finished it. Each person also rated how easy the task was, how quick it felt and how satisfied they were.

Your result

The usability score is 33%. It is the share of people who passed every check in every task they tried. Here, 4 of 12 people passed everything.

A person passes a task only when they finish it and rate ease, speed and satisfaction high enough. One low rating in one task is enough to fail.

No pass mark was agreed for this score, so it is not above or below any target. The true share is most likely between 14% and 61%.

What the score is made of

Each task has its own score, built from four checks. The checks have no targets of their own, so we report the plain counts.

Finding the nearest store scored 75%. Checking out scored 58%. Starting a return scored 42%.

  • Add to cart & checkout (58%): 10 of 12 people finished. 7 of 12 rated ease 4 or higher. 10 of 12 rated speed 3 or higher. 9 of 12 rated satisfaction 4 or higher.
  • Find nearest store (75%): 11 of 12 people finished. 10 of 12 rated ease 4 or higher. 11 of 12 rated speed 3 or higher. 10 of 12 rated satisfaction 4 or higher.
  • Start a return (42%): 7 of 12 people finished. 5 of 12 rated ease 4 or higher. 9 of 12 rated speed 3 or higher. 7 of 12 rated satisfaction 4 or higher.

How sure we are

With only 12 people, the true overall share is most likely between 14% and 61%. That is a wide range.

The task scores are just as uncertain. For starting a return, the true score is most likely between 19% and 68%.

Testing with more people would narrow these ranges. That matters most once a pass mark is set and the score has to be judged against it.

What to fix first

Starting a return is the weakest task. Only 5 of 12 people found it easy enough, and only 7 of 12 finished it.

In checkout, ease is also the weakest part: 7 of 12 people rated it 4 or higher. Look at where people hesitated in both tasks.

Finding the nearest store works best. Its weakest part is satisfaction, with 10 of 12 people rating it 4 or higher.

What this number cannot tell you

The score shows how many people got through everything. It does not say why the others struggled. Watch the recordings to find out.

The score ignores how long tasks actually took. It uses how quick each task felt, not the stopwatch. The time targets and the satisfaction target are not used either.

The result covers these three tasks and these 12 people only. Other tasks or other customers may do better or worse.

The answer also depends on the scoring method, as the next section explains.

Would another method change the answer?

Yes, by a lot. Other methods give partial credit for each part instead of a simple pass or fail, so they score higher.

The original four-part score, which also counts mistakes, gives 75%. The three-part version, which leaves out mistakes, gives 72%.

Averaging the percentages of the parts gives 68%. Setting time targets from how long satisfied people took gives 63%.

This study supports a go or no-go decision, and the pass-or-fail share fits that best. It asks how many people had a good enough experience on every check. A high average cannot hide one failed check.

Before the next round, agree a pass mark for this share. Then the score can be judged clearly against it.

Glossary

Usability score
The share of people who passed every check in every task they tried.
Check
One thing a person must get right in a task: finish it, and rate ease, speed and satisfaction high enough.
Pass mark
The share, agreed in advance, that the score must reach for the product to pass.
Range
The values the true score most likely falls between, given how few people took part.
Partial credit
A way of scoring that gives some points for a part done fairly well, instead of only pass or fail.
