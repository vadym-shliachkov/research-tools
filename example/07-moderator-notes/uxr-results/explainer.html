What we measured

Six people each tried two onboarding tasks for the team plan: Book a demo and Invite a teammate. During each call, a moderator noted whether they finished, how long it took, and how easy it felt.

A task counts as finished only if the person finished it without help. Someone who needed a hint from the moderator counts as not finished. People rated how easy each task felt from 1 to 5, where 5 means very easy. We use that rating as our measure of satisfaction.

The aim was to find the weaker of the two tasks, so the team knows where to look first.

Your result

The overall usability score is 45%. A score of 50% means exactly on target. Higher is better, and lower is worse.

The score looks a little below the 50% target, but the study cannot yet tell for sure. The likely range runs from 21% to 82%, and the target sits inside it.

Invite a teammate scored 42% and Book a demo scored 48%. For each task, we cannot yet tell whether it is above or below its target.

Invite a teammate scored lower, so it is the first candidate for the weaker task. But the two ranges overlap a lot, so this study cannot prove that it is weaker.

What the score is made of

Each task score combines three equal parts: how many people finished, how fast they were, and how easy it felt. Only the people who finished count towards time.

How sure we are

With six people, the true overall score is most likely between 21% and 82%. That range is very wide, because each person who finishes or does not finish moves the score a lot.

The task ranges are just as wide: 30% to 82% for Book a demo, and 21% to 76% for Invite a teammate. With so little data, even these ranges are only rough.

Testing more people would narrow the ranges. Only then could we say whether each task is above or below its target, and which task is really weaker.

What to fix first

For Invite a teammate, the weakest part is time. A typical attempt took 81 seconds against a target of 75 seconds. Look at which steps of inviting slow people down.

For Book a demo, the weakest part is satisfaction. People finished quickly enough, but the average ease rating was only 3.17 against a target of 4. Look at what made booking feel hard.

In each task, half of the people did not finish on their own. The moderator also noted that several people asked what "seat" means. That comes from the notes, not from a measured number, but the word is worth checking.

What this number cannot tell you

The score shows how well people did, not why they struggled. For the reasons, read the moderator's notes and watch any recordings.

A study of six people is very small, so every part of the score is uncertain. The times describe only the people who finished, because nobody else has a time recorded.

The data was copied by hand from notes typed during the calls, and the times were written as free text. The table was checked, but small copying or timing errors are still possible.

These results cover only these two tasks and these six people. Another common way of scoring gives 44%, so the choice of scoring method does not change the answer.

Glossary

Usability score
One number that sums up how well people managed a task. 50% means exactly on target; higher is better, lower is worse.
Target
The level the team agreed a task should reach, such as a time limit or an ease rating.
Range
The values the true score most likely falls between. Fewer people give a wider range.
Completion
Whether a person finished the task on their own, without a hint from the moderator.
Satisfaction
Here, the "How easy" rating each person gave after a task, from 1 to 5 where 5 means very easy.
Time on task
How long a typical person who finished took to complete the task.
